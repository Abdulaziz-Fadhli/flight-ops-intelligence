# Weather Operations Procedures

## When a Weather Hold Is Declared

A weather hold is declared by the duty operations manager when conditions
at the origin, destination, or en-route airspace fall below safe departure
or arrival minimums. All flights affected by the hold are marked with
status DELAYED and delay reason code WEATHER until the hold is lifted.

## Ground Stop vs. Weather Hold

- A **ground stop** halts all departures airport-wide, typically issued by
  air traffic control rather than the airline itself; affected flights
  should be recorded with delay reason code ATC, not WEATHER.
- A **weather hold** is airline-specific and applies only to flights the
  airline judges unsafe to dispatch, even if the airport itself remains
  open; these are recorded as WEATHER.

## Departure Sequencing After a Hold Lifts

When a weather hold lifts, flights depart in the order they were originally
scheduled, not in the order ground crews happen to finish preparing them.
Gate agents must not allow a later-scheduled flight to depart ahead of an
earlier-scheduled one solely because it is ready first, except when the
earlier flight has a mechanical (TECHNICAL) issue unrelated to the weather
hold itself.

## Passenger Communication During a Hold

Gate agents must issue a status update to affected passengers at least
every 30 minutes during an active weather hold, even if there is no change
to report. Silence during a hold is the single largest driver of passenger
complaints and should be treated as a service failure in its own right.

## Crew Duty Time Interactions

A prolonged weather hold can push a crew past its legal duty time limit
even if the aircraft itself is airworthy and ready to depart. If this
occurs, the flight's delay reason code should be updated from WEATHER to
CREW once the duty-time limit becomes the binding constraint, since further
delay at that point is no longer purely weather-caused.
